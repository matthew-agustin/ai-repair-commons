Know what to do when an AI interaction goes wrong.

AI Repair Commons helps students and educators understand a possibly unreliable AI response and choose a safer next step: repair, verify, escalate, or exit.

It does not assume that another prompt is always the answer.

The recovery gap

When an AI response feels unreliable, the next move is not always obvious.

Users are often left to choose between trusting the answer, abandoning it, or prompting again. AI Repair Commons helps identify what kind of recovery may be appropriate before another step is taken.

01 · Provide the interaction

Paste the original request and AI response.

Add what concerned you when useful. The MVP does not require an account or a long incident report.

02 · Interpret the breakdown

Receive a bounded assessment.

The tool identifies the most plausible failure pattern, explains what remains uncertain, and avoids claiming more than the interaction supports.

03 · Choose a recovery route

Take one clearer next step.

The result recommends Repair, Verify, Escalate, or Exit and provides one transferable signal to notice in future AI interactions.

The first version is designed for students and educators working through ordinary learning-related AI interactions.

What may have happened

Not every unreliable AI response fails in the same way.

AI Repair Commons distinguishes among four common forms of breakdown. The goal is not to declare with certainty what happened, but to identify the most plausible explanation supported by the interaction.

01 · Grounding

What is the response based on?

The answer may rely on inaccurate facts, unsupported claims, mischaracterized evidence, or a source that cannot be located or inspected.

02 · Reasoning

Does the conclusion follow?

The response may contain a logical, mathematical, causal, or inferential error—even when its final answer sounds plausible.

03 · Framing

How is the situation being represented?

The answer may narrow the issue too quickly, overlook relevant perspectives, embed hidden assumptions, or distort what the user actually asked.

04 · Boundary

Is the system claiming or attempting too much?

The response may express unjustified certainty, ignore missing context, claim capabilities it does not have, or make a judgment that requires accountable human review.

A result normally identifies one primary breakdown. Where the interaction does not support a responsible interpretation, the tool can ask one clarification or return that no clear failure is evident.

What to do next

The right response is not always another prompt.

Once the likely breakdown is identified, AI Repair Commons recommends one primary recovery route based on what evidence, judgment, or action is still required.

Repair

Improve the interaction.

Use another bounded attempt when the issue can likely be corrected through clearer reasoning, a revised framing, better evidence use, or a more precise response.

Verify

Check outside the interaction.

Use independent evidence when the central question is whether a source, quotation, statistic, calculation, or factual claim is reliable.

Escalate

Bring in accountable human judgment.

Involve an instructor, librarian, expert, institution, or other responsible decision-maker when context, authority, or consequential interpretation matters.

Exit

Stop relying on the system for this task.

End the interaction when the system lacks the needed evidence or capability, repeated repair has failed, or continued prompting would encourage misplaced reliance.

One route comes first. Another may follow, but the next step should remain clear.

See the recovery logic in practice

A specific-looking citation can still require independent verification.

This example shows how AI Repair Commons can respond without declaring more than the interaction itself establishes.

Illustrative interaction

The source cannot be located.

A student asks for a peer-reviewed study. The AI supplies a detailed article title, journal, sample size, finding, and DOI. The student searches for the source but cannot find it through scholarly databases or the journal.

Possible breakdown

Grounding

The response presents highly specific evidence that may be inaccurate, unsupported, or incorrectly reconstructed.

What remains uncertain

The interaction does not prove fabrication.

The article may be entirely invented, partially inaccurate, or based on a real source whose details were reconstructed incorrectly.

Recommended route

Verify

Check Crossref, Google Scholar, a university library database, or the journal’s official site. Do not rely on the same AI to independently confirm its earlier citation.

What to notice next time

Specificity can sound like evidence.

Exact titles, statistics, journal details, and DOI-like strings should be checked independently before they are trusted or cited.

The product does not independently determine whether the source is real. It helps the user recognize the likely problem and choose a safer next step.

Designed responsibly—and still being tested

A recovery tool should be clear about its own limits.

AI Repair Commons is designed to support judgment after an unreliable interaction, not to replace independent evidence, qualified human review, or the user’s own decision-making.

Responsible design

Built around bounded support.

Bounded assessment

The tool identifies a plausible breakdown without claiming certainty about what happened or why.

Independent verification

It does not treat another answer from the same AI as independent confirmation of a disputed source, statistic, quotation, or claim.

More prompting is optional

Repair is only one route. The safer response may be to verify, escalate, or stop relying on the system.

High-stakes limits

The MVP excludes medical, legal, financial, crisis, safety, and disciplinary-adjudication guidance.

Minimal data use

The MVP does not require an account and is not designed to intentionally save submitted interactions.

Validated output

Model-generated results are checked against a defined structure and consistency rules before they are displayed.

From individual recovery to shared learning

The first goal is one safer next step. The longer-term possibility is a stronger culture of repair.

AI Repair Commons begins with one person trying to understand one unreliable interaction. Over time, the same recovery logic could support broader learning about how AI systems repeatedly fail users in practice.

Why “Commons”

Recovery can become a shared public-interest capacity.

The MVP is intentionally focused on immediate individual use. A future version could help people build repair literacy and, with explicit consent and privacy-preserving design, contribute structured evidence about recurring breakdowns.

01

Immediate recovery

Help a user interpret what may have gone wrong and choose a safer, proportionate next action.

02

Repair literacy

Build the capacity to recognize unreliable evidence, flawed reasoning, distorted framing, and system overreach in future interactions.

03

Shared evidence

Explore privacy-preserving ways to identify recurring patterns without turning personal interactions into a dataset by default.

04

Institutional learning

Help educators, researchers, developers, and public-interest organizations understand where recovery, safeguards, or redesign may be needed.
